The Zakrajsheks who served.

From World War II to Korea, military service became part of this Minnesota family’s American story. Five Zakrajshek names appear on Sartell’s World War II memorial. One son was killed overseas. Another made the Navy a career. After losing her husband in combat, Vera joined the WAVES.

What you will not find here: invented ranks, imagined combat scenes or fake family portraits. Every detail is tied to a source; every gap stays visible.

The Sartell Historical Society identifies Joseph as a son of Frank Sr. and Matilda (Triller), born March 16, 1918. Its account and the Sacred Heart Cemetery transcription agree on his death date. The cemetery record identifies his rank and unit; the 1st Cavalry Division Association’s honor roll independently lists the same rank, unit and KIA date.

The archive does not claim an exact combat circumstance. That detail remains unwritten until a casualty file, unit history or wartime newspaper establishes it.

Edward J. “Zak” Zakrajshek

Born October 25, 1925 in Sartell, Edward served 22 years in the Navy and reached Chief Petty Officer. A published community profile reports service in both Atlantic and Pacific theaters during World War II, service in Korea from 1950–54 and a World War II Victory Medal.

Vera P. Zakrajshek

Vera was Joseph’s wife. A local history of Sartell’s veterans’ plaques reports that after learning Joseph had been killed, she joined the Women’s Reserve—commonly called the WAVES. Her name appears on Sartell’s WWII memorial roster.

Her rank, unit and service dates are not yet established. The archive is seeking her military file.

A necessary boundary

Thomas is not placed among those who served.

A cemetery transcription reports Thomas E. Zakrajshek as 1929–1944. A historical account of Joseph’s March 1945 death, however, names Thomas among the surviving brothers. Those claims conflict.

The archive is seeking an original death record and obituary. Until evidence resolves the discrepancy, no cause of death or military service is assigned to Thomas.

The shared record

Five names in bronze.

Sartell’s restored World War II plaque lists Anthony Zakrajshek, Edward Zakrajshek, Frank Zakrajshek Jr., Joseph Zakrajshek and Vera P. Zakrajshek. Joseph’s name bears a star identifying him among seven local people who died in the war.

The plaque is evidence of community remembrance. It is not a substitute for each person’s full military file.
