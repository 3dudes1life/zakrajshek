Supported

The surname is connected to place.

The Dictionary of American Family Names, reproduced by FamilySearch, defines Zakrajšek as an old Slovenian habitational name for someone from Zakraj in Inner Carniola, or a topographic name derived from zakraj, “farthest part of a place or village.” A scholarly Slovenian onomastics review also gives the derivation “Zakrajšek < Zakraj.”

  • FamilySearch, citing Dictionary of American Family Names ↗
  • ZRC SAZU / Jezikoslovni zapiski, surname derivation ↗

This explains a surname’s likely formation. It does not identify Frank’s village or parents.

Indexed lead

Zakrajšek records appear around Bloke by 1658.

The Slovenian Genealogical Society’s public name index includes Gregor Zakrajšek dated March 5, 1658 at Strmca, Bloke, plus later entries at Strmca, Zakraj, Nova Vas and elsewhere in the region. Its marriage index also contains many Bloke-area Zakrajšek entries in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Matricula, operated with the Archdiocesan Archive of Ljubljana, confirms that Bloke baptism register 00090 covers 1666–1680. Those digitized parish volumes are the right primary-source collection for checking index entries.

  • Slovenian Genealogical Society name index: Zakrajšek ↗
  • Slovenian Genealogical Society marriage index ↗
  • Matricula: Bloke baptism book 1666–1680 ↗

The 1658 item is currently an index entry, not a completed page-level transcription from the original register. It supports “nearly 370 years in the record trail,” but not “370+ years” yet.

Partly supported

Frank and Matilda’s American dates are visible; Frank’s origin is not.

A Sacred Heart Cemetery transcription lists Frank Zakrajshek (1893–1959) and Matilda Zakrajshek (1901–1997). A memorial-derived profile gives Frank’s full dates as October 27, 1893–May 23, 1959 and Matilda’s as February 25, 1901–January 31, 1997, and describes both as born in Yugoslavia. Those full dates still deserve confirmation through civil, church, obituary or Social Security records.

The same profile leaves Frank’s father and mother explicitly unknown. No reliable public source located in this audit identifies them or gives a specific Slovenian birthplace.

  • Sacred Heart Cemetery transcription ↗
  • Frank Zakrajshek profile and cited memorial ↗

Keep Frank’s parents, parish, village and house number unresolved. “Yugoslavia” is a historical-era place label from secondary records, not yet a precise birthplace.

Supported

Tony’s work and public service are visible in Sartell’s record.

A profile transcribing and citing Anthony “Tony” Zakrajshek’s October 31, 1987 St. Cloud Daily Times obituary states that he was born October 10, 1919 in Eveleth to Frank Sr. and Matilda (Triller) Zakrajshek, and died October 30, 1987. It records his Army Air Corps service and a 45-year career as an electrician for St. Regis Corporation—the Sartell paper mill.

Sartell Historical Society’s city history independently lists Tony as mayor from 1974 through 1978 and identifies him in a city-council photograph. The obituary adds his council service, volunteer firefighting and tenure as Sartell fire chief. Anna’s 2016 obituary identifies Anthony as her late husband and supports the later-generation family link.

  • Anthony Zakrajshek profile, transcribing and citing 1987 obituary ↗
  • Sartell Historical Society: mayor list and identified council photographs ↗
  • Sartell Historical Society: paper mill ownership timeline ↗
  • Anna M. Zakrajshek obituary, Newsleaders ↗

The verified office is mayor of Sartell, Minnesota—not Sauk Rapids. For a gold-standard archive, obtain Tony’s original obituary, employment record and a rights-cleared copy of the identified council photograph.

Supported context

Slovenian immigration is part of the documented Iron Range story.

The Minnesota Historical Society reports that Slovenes were among the Iron Range’s earliest immigrant groups and that Slovenes and Croats together formed more than 20 percent of the foreign-born population after settlement stabilized. Its collection includes a public-domain photograph of a Slovene wedding in Eveleth in 1908.

The Society’s page for the Slovenian-language newspaper Narodni Vestnik says Slovenes began arriving in Minnesota in the 1860s, first settling in Stearns County, with another wave drawn to northeastern Minnesota’s iron mining beginning in the mid-1880s.

  • MNopedia: Immigration to the Iron Range, 1880–1930 ↗
  • Minnesota Historical Society: Narodni Vestnik ↗

This is regional context, not proof that Frank mined or immigrated directly to Eveleth. His occupation and route still need personal records.

Supported

Joseph’s service and loss are publicly documented.

The Sartell Historical Society identifies Joseph Zakrajshek as a son of Frank Sr. and Matilda (Triller), born March 16, 1918. It records that he served as a technician fifth grade and was killed on Luzon in the Philippines on March 8, 1945 at age twenty-six. The 1st Cavalry Division Association’s honor roll independently lists “T-5 Zakrajshek, Joseph,” killed in action on the same date with the 8th Engineer Squadron. The Sacred Heart Cemetery transcription matches his birth date, death date, rank and unit.

  • Sartell Historical Society: Joseph Zakrajshek ↗
  • 1st Cavalry Division Association: WWII Book of Honor ↗
  • Sacred Heart Cemetery transcription ↗
  • U.S. War Department Minnesota honor list scan ↗

This is direct evidence for Joseph’s place in Frank and Matilda’s household and military history. It does not resolve Frank’s Slovenian origin.

Supported—with privacy limits

The surname remains present in the Bloke area.

The Municipality of Bloke’s public village-board page includes current Zakrajšek surname holders serving boards in Velike Bloke, Ravnik and Sveti Duh—the board that includes Strmca and Zakraj. This supports geographic continuity of the surname in the broader region.

  • Municipality of Bloke village boards ↗

It does not establish that any living person is related to William. The archive should not publish private contact information or call anyone a cousin without proof and consent.
